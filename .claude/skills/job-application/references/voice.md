# Luke's Voice

Calibration for anything written *as* Luke — cover letters, tech profiles, outreach. Derived from
his case-study prose in `src/pages/projects/*.mdx`, which is the source of truth, and from the
portfolio's own content standard in `case-study-intake.md`.

A letter in a plausible-but-wrong voice fails at the interview, when the person doesn't match the
page. Read the voice samples in `assets/evidence-bank.md` before drafting, not after.

## The five moves that make it sound like him

1. **Open on the concrete situation, not the abstraction.** "After a Saturday open home, an agent has
   a stack of business cards and a fading memory." Not "Adoption is a key challenge in field
   deployments."
2. **Name the alternative you rejected, and why.** This is the signature. Every architectural claim
   in his portfolio comes with the road not taken. In a cover letter it becomes: *here's what most
   people do, here's what I did instead, here's why.* It's the single fastest way to sound like
   someone with judgment rather than someone with a stack.
3. **Say the blunt thing.** "That is not data. That is the agent's gut feel, relabelled." He calls
   things what they are — theatre, decoration, a comfort blanket, a signature rather than a review.
   Do not sand this down into diplomatic mush.
4. **End on a real number.** Not a rounded-up one. `$138 → $0`, `16 mutating actions writing zero
   bytes`, `+612 / −0`, `0 false-passes at threshold across 10 items`. Specific odd numbers read as
   measured; round numbers read as marketing.
5. **Admit the limit before you're asked.** "Ten items is a small gold set, and it's the honest
   number." He pre-empts the sceptical follow-up rather than hoping it doesn't come. This *increases*
   credibility and is unusual enough to be a differentiator.

## Sentence mechanics

- **Vary length hard.** Long analytical sentence, then a four-word one. "That is not data."
- **Contractions: yes**, but not constantly. He writes "cannot" when the weight matters and "isn't"
  when it doesn't.
- **Assert, don't hedge.** No "I believe I would be able to." He states and then evidences.
- **Em-dashes: yes, genuinely.** His prose uses them heavily and it reads human, because the clauses
  aren't parallel and the sentence lengths around them vary. The generic-AI failure mode the cover
  letter reference warns about is the *triad* — three parallel clauses chained with dashes in a
  smooth rhythm. Avoid that specifically. Do not strip his em-dashes to satisfy the rule; that
  produces a letter in nobody's voice.
- **Italics for the pivot word**, sparingly: *plausible*, *structurally*, *actually*.

## Vocabulary

**Use:** production · ship · architecture · agentic · evals · pipeline · RAG · MCP · latency · cost ·
accuracy · single source of truth · fail-closed · gate · judgment · defensible

**Say:** users · customers · developers. **Never** "learners" — even in learning-role applications.
The portfolio standard is explicit about this and it's a positioning decision, not a style tic.

**Australian/British spelling** throughout: organisation, authorisation, prioritise, licence (noun),
behaviour. He writes this way; a letter in US spelling reads as templated.

**Avoid entirely:** passionate · dynamic · results-driven · innovative · synergy · leverage (as a
verb) · "I am writing to express" · "thrive in fast-paced environments" · "wear many hats" ·
"proven track record."

## The trap specific to his profile

He has a career transition in it — education and learning design into engineering and applied AI.
Two failure modes, both common:

- **Apologising for it.** "Although my background is in education…" Never. The transition is
  finished; the evidence is shipped production systems with real users and real money moving through
  them.
- **Hiding it.** Also wrong, and it wastes the strongest asset. Very few engineers can design a
  curriculum, run an SME interview, or write documentation a developer will actually read. For any
  developer-facing AI company, that combination is the differentiator, not the liability.

The correct frame is neither defence nor omission: **the teaching background is why the engineering
work is unusually well documented, well specified, and well explained** — and those are the same
skills that make specs canonical, make a rule file a permanent fix, and make an eval rubric
calibratable. State it as an advantage, in one sentence, and move on to the evidence.

## Register by audience

| Reader | Lead with | Keep short |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering hiring manager | the decision and the alternative rejected | the learning-design history |
| Non-technical recruiter | the business outcome and the number | the architecture |
| Founder / early-stage | shipped end-to-end, solo, with real users | the enterprise process work |
| L&D / enablement leader | the 0→9 system and the adoption metrics | the security engineering |

## Length discipline

Cover letter: 250–350 words, four paragraphs. Portal text box: 200. He writes densely, so the
instinct will be to keep going — cut instead. The density is the voice; the length is not.
