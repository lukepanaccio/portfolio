# Luke's Voice

Calibration for anything written *as* Luke — cover letters, tech profiles, outreach.

**Built from Luke's own writing** — the 3 Steps Away platform copy he wrote for an audience of
educated teenagers. **Not from the portfolio case studies: those were drafted by Claude and are not
a voice source.** If a draft starts sounding clipped, technical and blunt in the case-study way,
it's drifted to the wrong model — come back here.

The samples are informal and the applications won't be, so this file separates two things: the
**structural fingerprint** below is Luke's and carries into everything; the **register** is a dial
you turn up for a hiring manager. Copy the skeleton, not the exclamation marks.

## Voice source

## The structural fingerprint — this is Luke, keep it

1. **Open with one short, plain, true sentence, set off on its own.** "Big things are always made up
   of many small things." / "We live in a world that is changing faster than ever." He earns
   attention with a simple statement, then expands. No throat-clearing, ever.
2. **Bring himself in.** "Whenever I'm feeling overwhelmed by a particular project, I settle my
   anxiety down by…" / "Personally, it has felt like I'm watching a history textbook play out in real
   time." He writes from a personal stake and a concrete lived moment, not from a podium.
3. **Talk to the reader.** Second person, real questions, invitation. "How can you break this down
   into manageable chunks?" The writing feels aimed at one person and expects a response.
4. **Isolate the pivot in italics on its own line.** "*But we can affect change.*" / "*Justice is
   fairness.*" The turn of the argument gets its own short italic line. This is his signature and it
   survives formalisation untouched.
5. **Explain by grounding, not abstracting.** The dopamine hit of striking a task off a list; food
   waste as "the third biggest emitter of carbon after the USA and China." He reaches for the
   concrete, relatable thing. In a letter this becomes: prove the claim with the specific event and
   the real number, never the adjective.
6. **State conviction plainly.** "At 3 Steps Away, we don't wonder; we know it is worthwhile." No
   "I believe I could." He asserts, then backs it.
7. **Build by accretion, not by triads.** Sentences open with "And", "But", "So". Long sentences grow
   clause-by-clause joined with "and" — the opposite of the machine-smooth parallel-triad rhythm.
   Keep this; it's also *why* his em-dashes read human — the clauses around them are uneven and
   alive.

## Mechanics

- **Contractions: yes, throughout.** "it's", "we're", "don't", "can't". He does not write "cannot"
  for gravitas — that was the Claude case-study voice. Keep him contracted.
- **Sentence length: vary hard, lean flowing.** A three-to-six-word line for the turn, then a longer
  accreting sentence. Not uniformly clipped.
- **Semicolons, used loosely and a little idiosyncratically.** "It's challenging; but as Micah
  Bournes speaks below, it's also very much rewarding." Fine to keep one; don't litter a formal
  letter with them.
- **Em-dashes: genuinely his** — conversational, not the parallel-triad the cover-letter reference
  warns against. The triad is the thing to avoid; his uneven dashes are not that, so don't strip
  them.
- **Australian spelling** throughout: colour, colonisation, realise, centre, programme, prioritise.
- **Repetition for rhythm:** "people, all people, all around the world"; "over and over". Sparingly
  in a letter, but it's his.

## Register — the dial you turn UP for applications

The samples are warm, earnest and pitched at teenagers. For a hiring manager, turn *these* down and
leave the fingerprint above untouched:

- **Exclamation marks** → near-zero. His "ask your parents!" warmth becomes quiet confidence, not
  enthusiasm on the page.
- **Chatty asides to a teenage reader** → cut. Keep the occasional dry aside only if it earns its
  place.
- **World-saving earnestness** → a letter's conviction is about the work, not a cause. Same
  certainty, aimed at "here's what I'd build for you."
- **The opener stays short and plain, but lands on the employer's problem**, not a universal truth.
  "You're hiring someone to keep learning content in step with a product that ships weekly" is the
  formal cousin of "Big things are made up of many small things."

**The test:** read the finished letter aloud. If it sounds like a warmer, more direct human than a
template — one plain opener, a personal stake, a concrete number, one italic pivot — the dial is
right. If it sounds like a campaign speech, turn it further down. If it sounds like *nobody*, the
register got stripped out along with the structure — start again from the fingerprint.

## Vocabulary

**Use** (engineering register): production · ship · architecture · agentic · evals · pipeline · RAG ·
MCP · latency · cost · accuracy · single source of truth · fail-closed · gate · judgment ·
defensible.

**Say** users · customers · developers for his professional audience. **Not "learners"**, even in a
learning-role application — it's a positioning decision. (Note the distinction: his teenage-facing
copy addresses "you" warmly, and that *mode of address* is worth keeping; the *label* "learners" is
the thing to avoid.)

**Avoid entirely:** passionate · dynamic · results-driven · innovative · synergy · leverage (verb) ·
"I am writing to express" · "thrive in fast-paced environments" · "wear many hats" · "proven track
record."

## The transition — his strongest, most mishandled asset

He moved from education and learning design into engineering and applied AI. Two failure modes:

- **Apologising for it.** "Although my background is in education…" Never. The transition is finished;
  the evidence is shipped production systems with real users and real money.
- **Hiding it.** Also wrong, and it wastes the best card. Very few engineers can write copy a
  fourteen-year-old will actually read, run an SME interview, or design a curriculum.

The right frame is neither: **the teaching background is why the engineering is unusually well
explained, well specified and well documented** — the same instinct that makes a plain opener land
also makes specs canonical and eval rubrics calibratable. And the voice itself is the proof: he can
make a hard thing clear to someone who doesn't already understand it, which is exactly what
developer-facing and forward-deployed work needs. State it as an advantage, once, then move to the
evidence.

## Register by audience

| Reader | Lead with | Keep short |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering hiring manager | the decision and what you'd build | the learning-design history |
| Non-technical recruiter | the outcome and the number | the architecture |
| Founder / early-stage | shipped end-to-end, solo, with real users | the enterprise process work |
| L&D / enablement leader | the 0→9 system and the adoption metrics | the security engineering |

## Length discipline

Cover letter: 250–350 words, four paragraphs. Portal text box: 200. His natural mode is expansive and
accreting, so the instinct will be to keep going — cut. The warmth and the structure are the voice;
the length is not.
