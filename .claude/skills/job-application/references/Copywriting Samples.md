# **Sample 1**

## **TITLE: OUR FOCUS**

Big things are always made up of many small things. 

Whenever I'm feeling overwhelmed by a particular project, I settle my anxiety down by breaking down the project into smaller chunks and turning them into a list of some sort. Focusing on these smaller tasks, which are much easier to manage and work on, allows me to make progress and to chip away at the larger task. 

I've done this for many things; building websites, creating businesses, designing courses, writing music, having and supporting a family. It's the same process over and over. 

And it's a tiny thing, but striking that pen through a task on the list (or changing its colour in an online doc) gives that little dopamine reward we need to encourage us to keep going. 

With a project like 3 Steps Away, where we are looking at the great problems of our world today and how to best solve them, this strategy is especially important. It's one of those things you will hear us repeat over and over; how can you break this down into manageable chunks? How are these smaller components connected? And sometimes that can mean trying to identify what we can't initially see.

We had to decide what issues we wanted to focus on with our platform, as there are too many things in our world needing fixing to do it all at once. We came up with a list of four to kick things off with; the Climate Crisis, Racism, Misinformation and Misogyny. These four issues are integral to the kind of justice we want to see in the world today.

**Why these 4 issues?**

*The Climate Crisis*  
Our position is that the climate crisis needs to be our primary focus. It's the great challenge of our time, and not dealing with it is a direct threat to the very survival of our species. All other issues have links to it as well, which we hope will become evident the more you spend time with us here. There is no doubt at 3 Steps Away that the climate crisis is real. That discussion for us is closed. Our take is that we now need to be part of the solution. Many of the solutions already exist but the political will to put these solutions into place has been lacking. That's where much of the work now lies.

*Racism*  
Racism is a complex topic, but we think it's imperative we address it as a matter of urgency. All around the world, people of colour and indigenous peoples continue to suffer discrimination in many forms. The effects of centuries of imperialism and colonisation continue to need addressing, and much work remains to rectify the injustice caused by slavery, even in its modern form. In Australia, we have decided to focus on indigenous incarceration as an example of systemic and institutional racism. 

*Misinformation*  
For many misinformation may be a new topic to start thinking about. But at 3 Steps Away we think that this is becoming more and more an issue that needs immediate address. We live in a world where information surrounds us everywhere. Not just in public spaces, but also in our private worlds. And much of this information is deliberately designed to change the way our thinking operates. We saw this play out dramatically during the Covid 19 pandemic, but it has been part of the landscape for a long time in the form of propaganda and even advertising. The survival of our democratic institutions and way of life requires us to be aware of how misinformation spreads and to notice its effects.

*Misogyny*  
Along with people of colour, women continue to experience discrimination in many forms. Much of this stems from misogyny; the control of women by men . From being the victims of sexual and domestic abuse, to being locked out of education, women around the world continue to be the victims of established patterns of oppression. There is a long history of women fighting to improve their situation in society, but much work remains to be done.

**Tip of the iceberg**

These issues really are just the tip of the iceberg. This list is by no means complete.  
Maybe you are more concerned about other social justice topics? Does that mean that they are off the table for discussion here at 3 Steps Away? Absolutely not. It's our hope that as this community grows, other issues will become key focus areas as well. We just needed a starting point and identified those that we think are the best places for us to start.

On the menu on the left of your screen you can see a menu item called Topics, which leads to a summary of the topics listed above. Click through those topics and follow those that interest you, so that your activity feed here will prioritise any new updates.  
We are really looking forward to exploring these topics with you\! And we can't wait to see your ideas for how we can best address them.

## **TITLE: 3 STEPS AWAY FROM A MORE JUST WORLD**

We live in a world that is changing faster than ever.

It's not just young people who are feeling it. Old people are feeling it too (ask your parents\!). The new information technologies from the last few decades have sped everything up, and alongside that we are witnessing the fracturing of the world created after world war 2\. 

It seems like everywhere we look, there is a new and bigger crisis. It can feel overwhelming. It can feel like none of us have any power to change even a small bit of it; it's all just so big.

*But we can affect change.* 

We are still able to do our part to solve some of the great problems of our time. 3 Steps Away is a platform that is all about an education in the principle of justice, primarily social justice, and how it can be used to empower people, people like you, to become change-makers in the world.

**What is justice?**

Justice is one of those words that is super tricky to have a simple and clear cut definition, even though Google will try and give you one when you search for "justice definition". 

The first two minutes of the video above does a pretty good job at showing the complexity of the term. But it also points to what it is that makes justice simple to understand too, and what most people can understand: 

*Justice is fairness.* 

It's doing what is right.  
We have a pretty clear sense of what it means to be fair from when we are little. "Muuuuuuuuuummmm, that's not fair\!" Little kids can be heard around the world making their case for fairness. 

But one of the lessons that we learn as we get older and older is that the world is not fair, and that there are both winners and losers in this world, and that some people are born into a position where there is an extreme lack of fairness in their chance at the game of life. 

Justice is all about trying to rectify that imbalance, and to make sure that people, all people, all around the world, get their fair share to make a beautiful life.

Living a life with justice at its centre is challenging; but as Micah Bournes speaks below, it's also very much rewarding.

3 Steps: See. Think. ACT.

3 Steps Away from a more just world.

## **TITLE: HUMAN NATURE \- ARE WE INHERENTLY GOOD?**

Many people will wonder if doing what is right, and seeking fairness and justice for others is a worthwhile pursuit. 

At 3 Steps Away, we don't wonder; we know it is worthwhile.

This contradicts one of the myths that we are often told about human nature; that we are inherently selfish, lazy or worse. One of the great stories that has perpetuated this myth is William Golding's classic novel Lord of the Flies, which tells the story of children marooned on a desert island and how their worst instincts come out when they are freed of the demands of civilisation.

Rutger Bregman, in his book Humankind, researched a real life version of this story. 6 young boys become shipwrecked on a Pacific island. Rather than their worst instincts coming to play out, the exact opposite took place. You can read about it here. 

This news report also has some interviews and vision of those young men involved.

What is your understanding of human nature? Are we by nature selfish, or are we actually pretty decent? Tell us in the comments in the below.

## **TITLE: GLOBAL GOALS \- FOOD**

Food, glorious food.

One of the critical facts that is mentioned in this episode of An Idiot's Guide to Saving the World is that in terms of global carbon emissions, if food wastage was an actual country, it would be the third biggest emitter of carbon after the USA and China. That's a crazy thought. So much food is being wasted, and still there is so much hunger across the planet.

Zero hunger is Global Goal number 2\. It's a critical issue that is being further and further compounded each year. The impacts of the Covid 19 pandemic, war, rising costs of living, supply chain issues, and not to mention (yet again) climate change, are all having huge impacts on global food security. You may have heard this term before, maybe not; basically food security means making sure that our food production systems are robust and resilient enough that we are able to feed our populations.

A lot is covered in this episode, from hearing about how the UN's World Food Program and the work they do around the world trying to help people who are starving, to how we can make sure food is not wasted in our cities, and how we all need to relearn how to grow food and regenerate our soils (which have become lifeless in so many parts of the world and incapable of growing food).

A really interesting interview is done with an Indigenous American chef who is making contemporary cuisine using native foods. This reminded me of the work Bruce Pascoe has been doing with using local grains to make bread in Australia. Rediscovering the foods that existed on land that has been colonised is important and fascinating work.

Some of the other actions mentioned in the episode: 

* Buying local and seasonal food.   
* Voting for politicians who look at solving the food problem, or lobbying the ones we have to do so.  
* And looking at reducing meat in our diet, as well as making sure you are not wasting food.

The take action page for Global Goal 2 also lists a bunch of actions that you can take as well.
